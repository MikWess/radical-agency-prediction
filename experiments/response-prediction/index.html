Experiment 01 / Personal response prediction

Can a model predict
what I’ll say?

Short questions. Off-the-cuff answers. A growing set of examples of how you respond—and a fair test of whether a model learns anything from them.

Try a predictionLocal model · Hidden prediction, then reveal
The question isn’t whether it can imitate your phrasing. It’s whether it can anticipate the substance of an answer it hasn’t seen.

Working plan · open to revision

A small experiment we can actually learn from.

Collect naturally

Answer a few short questions at a time. Say what comes to mind. Keep the original words, question, date, and optional context.

Predict before revealing

Give a model earlier examples, then a new question. Save its prediction before showing it your real answer. Keep the prediction hidden while you answer.

Compare and repeat

Judge whether it got your main point and important details. Try more examples, then fine-tuning, using fresh questions for each evaluation.

The live experiment

Answer first. Then see its guess.

Connecting…

A local Qwen3 4B model predicts using your earlier answers. Its guess is saved before you answer and hidden until you reveal it. Each new answer becomes evidence for the next question. This is learning from context, not weight training.

Keep the test honest

What we decide before the first run.

Start small, and reserve genuinely new questions

Proposal: collect 30–50 short answers across a few sessions, then test 15–20 new questions. These are pilot targets, not a guarantee of sufficient training data. Keep rephrasings of the same question together so near-duplicates don’t leak into the test.

Separate prediction from influence

Lock every prediction before revealing your answer to the model. Don’t show yourself predictions until your own answer is saved. Once a test answer becomes a training example, retire it from future held-out evaluations.

Track change over time

Keep dated answers, including disagreements with earlier answers. For later runs, only use examples available before that run. Repeat a few questions in separate sessions to understand how consistent your own responses are.

What is connected now, and what comes next

The live loop runs Qwen3 4B on this Mac, records its prompt, settings, example IDs and prediction time, and reveals its guess only after your answer is saved. It uses up to 40 recent answers within a size limit, excluding prior answers to the same question. It does not update model weights. Next: compare with a no-examples baseline on the same fresh questions.

Keep personal evidence separate from public code

The public repository contains this page and the method. Answers, transcripts, predictions, and model files stay in ignored private directories. The browser talks only to a loopback server on this Mac. No analytics, remote fonts, third-party scripts, or hosted inference are used.
